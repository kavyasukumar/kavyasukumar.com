# Transit map rules

Rules for the career transit map on the dataviz page. The map is redrawn from scratch against these rules.

Rules 1–8 are the core rules and were set by the map's owner. Rules 9 and on are derived from three reference maps and are proposals until approved:

- **NYC72**: the 1972 Vignelli New York subway map.
- **NYC08**: the 2008 Vignelli-style New York subway map.
- **LON**: the London Underground map (Beck tradition).

All sizes are in **lw**, one line width (the width of a single track).

## Vocabulary

- **Line**: one colour. A line is a facet of the career, such as code, journalism or investing. Line colours: journalism Pink #E39BAE, code Forest #0B3D2E, leadership and outreach Raspberry #B0466F, product Mint #9AD9B0; not drawn yet: investing Ink #0A0A0A. Gold is unused. [owner decision]
- **Track**: one theme of a line, drawn in the line's colour. A track and a theme are the same thing: a line can have more than one track, one per theme. For example, the code line has "for the love of code" and "code as a tool for social justice". A track is named and labelled by its theme letter only (for example "A"): one capital letter per track, assigned per line (journalism A B C, code D E F G, product H I J, leadership K L; not drawn yet: investing M N). The letters were set before leadership moved above product, so the ribbon order is A B C | D E F G | K L | H I J. [owner decision]
- **Theme**: a named thread of the career, such as "Leadership". It is the entry in the key (rule 33) and is exactly one track. Names of themes are working names.
- **Branch**: one arm of a track after it splits (rule 5). Where a theme's work runs in parallel, its track splits and each concurrent role gets its own branch, because a branch cannot be in two places at once. All branches of a track are still that one track: same letter, same name.
- **Bundle (ribbon)**: two or more tracks that run side by side between the same stops. The map has one ribbon. Top to bottom (left to right in the vertical layout) it runs journalism A B C, then code D E F G, then leadership K L, then product H I J: `A B C | D E F G | K L | H I J`, with `LINE_GAP` between lines (rule 11). Leadership goes above product (LD1); before that it sat at the bottom. [owner decision]
- **Stop**: any place on the map where a track stops to let people on or off. A stop is a station, a terminal or an interchange (rule 7).
- **R1, R2, R3 (rows)**: reference names for the horizontal rows of the map. R1 (top) holds Bad Idea Factory and Uni. of Washington. At Bad Idea Factory the 7 journalism and code tracks make one concentric turn north together, and product H joins them from the west (rule 20, back-branch); the 8 dots form one row, H | A B C | D E F G, joined by a straight Ink connector, and all 8 run on north, dashed, off the top edge. Uni. of Washington is a north J-curve fan (A B C | E F G | L) whose square terminals sit level with the Bad Idea Factory dots, with its label above. There is no leadership at Bad Idea Factory. R2 (middle) is the full-time ribbon. R3 (bottom) holds the other short gigs: Seattle Globalist (K L | J) and All Things Small (B C | D E | K | H I) as J-curves going south, and Lighthouse Reports (A B C | D E F G | L) going south-east then east. There is no south UW fan. **Owner decision:** on desktop (horizontal layout) R1, R2 and R3 are each about 20% taller than the base spacing: R1 by the turn and dot lanes of the north fans, R2 by the gap between the ribbon and its label band (1.2x the label gap), R3 by the drop and the dot lanes of the south fans. The vertical (phone) layout keeps the base spacing (rule 40). [owner decision] **Owner decision (LD4):** on desktop R1, R2 and R3 get another ~20% taller. Desktop values (lw): BIF/UW turn lane -9.4 and dot row -17.2 (R1); label gap 1.44x (R2); drop 3.2, south gig dots at lane 25.7, Lighthouse dot column centred at lane 20.9 (R3). ## Core rules

1. Each line is one colour and represents a facet, such as code, journalism or investing.
2. A line may have more than one track. Tracks of the same colour are called tracks, not lines. The branches of one track (rule 5) are still one track, not several.
3. When a station has more than one track, those tracks stay next to each other.
4. The layout minimises overlaps and crossings. Tracks of one line may cross where their splits require it, keeping crossings to a minimum; every crossing follows rules 8, 18 and 19. [owner decision]
5. A track may split into branches where its theme's work runs in parallel. It splits just after a stop, never on one, and each branch peels off with concentric bends (rule 14). Branches of one track never cross each other and never rejoin. A track can also turn, tracks can come together into a bundle and move apart again, and a new track can be wedged into a bundle at an interchange. [owner decision; replaces 'a track cannot split']
6. A track can turn 45° or 90°, in any direction.
7. A stop is one of three kinds:
   - **Station**: one track passes through it.
   - **Terminal**: one track stops there. It is the end of that track. Only work that has ended gets a terminal (rule 27).
   - **Interchange**: two or more tracks go to it, whether the tracks are the same colour or different colours.
8. Where tracks overlap or cross, the tracks going straight stay on top. This applies consistently: at a given turn, every straight-through track is on top of every turning track. The turning tracks are the ones cut by the casing (rule 19).

## Derived rules

### Bundles and spacing

9. **Tracks that share a corridor form a bundle.** Draw them parallel and keep their left-to-right order along the whole shared stretch. Tracks never swap places inside a bundle. [NYC08, LON]
10. **Tracks of the same colour touch, split by a hairline of background colour.** The hairline is about 0.12 lw, so the track pitch is about 1.12 lw. [NYC08, LON]
11. **Tracks of different colours sit at the same hairline as tracks of one colour.** There is one uniform pitch of about 1.12 lw everywhere, with no wider gap between colours. Colour separates the lines. [NYC full map, NYC08, LON] **Owner decision:** wherever two lines sit side by side (journalism and code, code and leadership, leadership and product: the ribbon, Bad Idea Factory, Lighthouse Reports, the gig fans), the gap between the two lines is `LINE_GAP` = 0.25 lw, the same everywhere; the Ink interchange connector crosses that gap. At Bad Idea Factory product H sits one `LINE_GAP` west of journalism A. Within one line the hairline pitch stays and the dots touch. [replaces 'no wider gap between colours' between lines]
12. **Every line is the same width everywhere**, in flat colour with no gradients. [NYC72, NYC08, LON]

### Angles and turns

13. **Use only 0°, 45° and 90° for tracks, connectors and label baselines** (labels stay at 0°). This is rule 6 applied to everything on the map. [NYC72, NYC08, LON]
14. **A bundle turns in concentric arcs that share one centre.** The inner track has a radius of about 1.5 lw and each next track adds one track pitch. Track order and the gap between tracks are the same through the turn. [NYC08, LON]
15. **Round every bend. Never put a stop on a bend.** Leave a straight run of at least 1.5 lw before and after each arc. [NYC08, LON]
16. **Use 45° runs for long moves only.** A 45° run is at least about 10 lw long. A short sideways shift is a 45° jog of about 2–4 lw. [NYC08]
17. **Each track bends rarely.** Change direction to reach a corridor or an interchange, then hold that direction for a long run. Aim for at most one bend per three stops, and count bends per track as a check. [LON]

### Crossings and branches

18. **Tracks cross at 90° or 45° only.** A crossing never sits on, or within about 2.5 lw of, a stop that the crossing track does not serve. The layering follows rule 8. This includes tracks of the same line: a branch that splits off one track may cross the other tracks of its line on its way out (rule 4). [LON, NYC08; owner decision for same-line crossings]
19. **Show a crossing plainly.** There is no bridge symbol and no dot. The top track gets a casing of background colour on each side, the same width as the hairline between parallel tracks (about 0.12 lw, rule 10). **Owner decision:** the casing equals the hairline, not 0.25 lw. A side that faces the outside of its bundle (no parallel neighbour one pitch away) gets no casing, and the casing only cuts the track being crossed. [NYC08, LON, owner decision] **Owner decision (low/over crossings):** a track can be marked `low`: it is drawn in a layer beneath every other track, and the tracks marked `over` it get the usual cream casing where they cross it. Product H's back-branch (rule 20) is `low`, and the Bad Idea Factory bundle's north-east diagonals pass over it with their casings, so the Mint goes under them.
20. **A branch is an arm of a split track.** It leaves its track just after a stop (rule 5) and peels off with concentric bends. Put it on the side of the track it leaves toward, so it never crosses a sibling branch. The bend sits at least about 2 lw from the nearest stop. [LON] **Owner decision:** part-time work branches off its track this way instead of starting abruptly. Gig branches leave the full-time trunk, never an ongoing part-time branch: for journalism, Lighthouse Reports' branches split off the trunk at Washington Post (where the trunk ends) and run on to Lighthouse, and no branch leaves Bad Idea Factory. **Owner decision:** Lighthouse Reports' branches leave the trunk heading south-east at 45°, lowest theme first, turn east with concentric bends and run straight to the Lighthouse stop on a run below the trunk; they never go above the trunk or loop over it. Its dots touch in a column on the east run; it is a pure terminal, so they are squares and the tracks end at them (rule 26), and its label sits right of the dots, vertically centred (south-running in the vertical layout: label under the dots). [owner decision] [owner decision] **Owner decision:** a single-stop branch (one that serves exactly one stop and ends there, such as All Things Small or Uni. of Washington) is a J-curve, not an S-curve: it leaves its parent with one concentric bend and runs perpendicular to the parent, with no second bend back to parallel. Its terminal (rule 26) sits at the far end of the perpendicular leg, after a straight run of at least 1.5 lw past the arc (rule 15). Its label sits at the tip, in the direction of travel: under the dot for a leg running south, over it for north, right of it for east, left of it for west, 0.5–1 lw past the track end, which at a pure terminal is the square's edge (rules 26, 30). This holds in both layouts; in the vertical layout the legs run east or west. J-curves of one stop fan off a shared bundle as concentric bends, so the stop's dots touch. If two legs or their labels would collide, lengthen one leg or flip it to the other side. Multi-stop and ongoing branches keep their shape. **Owner decision (back-branch):** product's trunks start abruptly at Hearken (no run-on west of it). Product H reaches Bad Idea Factory by branching BACK from Hearken: it starts at Hearken's H dot, runs a short straight west, climbs north-west at 45° through the ribbon trunks, then turns north as the WEST leg of the Bad Idea Factory row (H | A B C | D E F G), so the other stations do not shift. It passes under the Bad Idea Factory diagonals (rule 19, low/over). Seattle Globalist's J peels off the trunk after Hearken as a south J-curve. The Lighthouse Reports fan crosses the product trunks on its way south-east, because product is ongoing. **Owner decision (leadership, LD1-LD3):** K (Team) is a trunk from Palm Beach Post to Aspada, ongoing (dashed off the edge), with stops at Hearken and LightRock India and gig J-curves to Seattle Globalist and All Things Small. L (Evangelism) is a trunk from OpenNews to the Lighthouse split just after Washington Post, where it continues as its Lighthouse Reports branch (the lowest one, so it leaves first); Hearken is a circle stop on it, and its Seattle Globalist and Uni. of Washington (north) gigs peel off the trunk. There is no leadership at Bad Idea Factory. Gig branches of one track that leave the same trunk are siblings even where their departure stubs overlap, so neither makes the other's terminal mixed (rule 26). Proposal: where a J-curve has to cross other tracks of its line to reach its leg, its 90° turn is drawn as two 45° arcs with the crossing on the 45° run between them (rule 18); in the vertical layout, a J-curve's tip label sits under its dots, so it never crosses a track or runs off a phone-width canvas (rule 29).
21. **Wedge a new track or branch into a bundle at an interchange.** A branch here is an arm of a split track (rule 20) and follows the same rule. The joining track arrives with a concentric turn (rule 14), takes its place next to its neighbours with the same-colour hairline (rule 10), and the stop where it joins is an interchange with a dot for every track in the bundle. The bundle keeps its track order, so the new track goes on the side it came from. The same applies in reverse when a track leaves. Tracks of one colour may also arrive from different directions and merge into one bundle, as the 4 and 5 do at 149 St. [NYC08, NYC full map]

### Stops

22. **Every track or branch that stops gets its own dot, centred on it.** The dot is black and about 1 lw across, the same as the track width, and never wider than the track. It carries the track's letter in white, and nothing else (one capital letter, no numbers or suffixes): every dot on a track or any of its branches shows the same letter. The letter is centred in the dot both ways, on its optical centre. Same-colour tracks are told apart by their dots. [NYC08, NYC full map] **Owner decision:** the track width (lw) is set so the ID in a dot is at least 12px; on screen a track is never narrower than 16px. **Owner decision (accepted exception):** with journalism and code on one map, station gaps are tightened to fit 1440 px, so the width there is about 14.4 px per lw (layout `minLw` 12), below the 16 px minimum. The owner accepted this. **Owner decision (accepted exception, phone):** in the vertical (phone) layout lw may drop below this rule so nothing is clipped, down to a floor of about 8 px per lw. Below the floor the map frame (the map's container, not the page) scrolls sideways instead of the map shrinking further; the page itself never scrolls sideways. **Owner decision:** every dot has a cream ring, because Ink dots disappear on dark tracks (Forest code now, Ink investing later). **Owner decision:** a hovered, keyboard-focused or selected station keeps Ink dots, grown to about 1.2x around their own centres, with each dot's letter in its own track's line colour instead of white (at an interchange of several lines, each letter takes its own line's colour); its interchange connector stays Ink. This overrides the grey-out (rule 41) for that station only. **Owner decision (dark-line rule):** a dark line gets a Cream letter on highlight instead of its line colour. Dark means the line colour's WCAG contrast against Ink is below 4.5:1, for example Forest (code), Raspberry (leadership, 3.73:1) and Ink (investing); Pink (9.02:1) and Mint (12.19:1) keep their own colour. **Owner decision (option c):** the cut-off was raised from 3:1 to 4.5:1 so K and L take Cream letters. This is computed from the line colour, not set per line name, so a new line follows it automatically.
23. **A track that skips a stop passes with no mark** (no dot, no gap, no change of width). Only the tracks that stop get dots. [NYC08, LON]
24. **An interchange is a set of dots, one per track, always joined by a thin Ink connector.** The connector runs centre to centre through all of the station's dots, is about 0.14 lw thick (0.12–0.15 lw), and follows 0°, 45° or 90°. It is drawn even when the dots touch: at the standard 1.12 lw pitch it shows across the hairline gap between neighbouring dots. It sits above the tracks and under the dots, and it joins separate bundles when the dots cannot touch. When a theme is highlighted (rule 41) it greys with the station: grey when none of the station's tracks are highlighted, Ink otherwise. The key's Interchange symbol shows the same connector. [owner decision; replaces "no connector needed when the dots touch"; NYC08, NYC72]
25. **The dots of an interchange do not have to sit on one straight line.** Each dot stays centred on its own track, wherever that track is. The tracks of an interchange may be on different horizontals, verticals or diagonals, and may approach from different directions. The connector bends to follow them, along 0°, 45° or 90°, with rounded corners. It can be straight, L-shaped or U-shaped, and it never crosses a track it does not serve. Dots that touch stay touching. For example, B and D stack vertically with the 4 offset to the side, and the 4 and 5 are joined by a U-shaped connector under the 2. [NYC full map] **Exceptions (owner decisions, re-listed after leadership moved above product, LD1/LD2):** the owner accepted these four connectors that cross a track passing through the station without a stop: (1) Palm Beach Post, C to E crosses code D (option A); (2) Palm Beach Post, E to K crosses code G; (3) OpenNews, G to L crosses leadership K; (4) LightRock India, K to J crosses product H and I. The earlier LightRock India exception (code F to product J across H and I) is gone: F now joins K, with no track between them.
26. **A terminal is a square; the track ends at the station.** At a pure terminal, a stop where every track at the stop ends, each dot is drawn as a square instead of a circle: the same size as the dot (side 1 lw), the same Ink fill and white track letter, sharp corners. Each track stops at its square's centre, so the square covers the end and no track shows past it: no overrun, no ring, bar, arrow or ID disc. At a multi-dot pure terminal the squares touch as the circles would, and the interchange connector still applies (rules 24, 25). Mixed stops, where some tracks end while a branch split off them runs on past the stop (Washington Post, where the trunk ends while the Lighthouse Reports branches run on), keep circles and end their tracks about 0.6 lw past the last dot. **Owner decision (per-track terminal):** the rule applies per track. At a mixed stop, a track that truly ends there gets its own square and ends at the square's centre with no overrun, while the other tracks at the stop keep their circles. A track truly ends at a stop when it is not ongoing, the stop is its last, and no branch carries it on past the stop. A branch carries it on only if it leaves the track at that stop (as the Lighthouse branches leave the trunks where they end at Washington Post); a branch that left at an earlier stop is its own track. Example: code F (code as a tool for social justice) at LightRock India is a square, because its Lighthouse branch left at Washington Post; E, K and J there keep circles. Washington Post keeps circles, because every trunk ending there continues as a Lighthouse branch. Station hover, focus and selected states apply to squares as to circles (rule 41). The pure terminals are Seattle Globalist, All Things Small, Uni. of Washington and Lighthouse Reports; LightRock India is a mixed stop with one square (F). [owner decision; replaces "ends square about 0.3–1 lw past its last dot" and the NYC ID disc]
27. **Ongoing work runs off the map in its direction of travel, dashed past its last stop; no TODAY label, no end mark.** This applies per branch. A track or branch for work that is still continuing does not end in a terminal (rule 26). It is solid to just past its last stop (about 0.6 lw), then carries straight on in the direction it was travelling, dashed (same colour and width; about 1 lw dash, 0.5 lw gap, butt caps, starting with a gap), and leaves the edge of the map canvas, with no end disc, no stop mark at the edge and no TODAY label. The last stop on the branch (for example Aspada or Bad Idea Factory) is an ordinary station or interchange. In the vertical layout the run leaves in the rotated direction (north becomes west). The key's "Ongoing" symbol shows a short solid track and dot, then dashes cut at the swatch edge. [owner decision; replaces "runs to the edge that is today"]
28. **Space stops evenly within a straight run.** Do not space by true time. Typical gap about 12 lw, minimum about 8 lw. Within one run, gaps vary by 25% or less. Dense areas may take about 20 lw to leave room for labels. [LON] **Owner decision:** only order matters, not timing, so every station gets the same slot in career order.

### Labels

29. **Labels are horizontal and sit on the same side along a straight run.** They switch side only at a bend or to avoid a collision. A label never crosses a track, a dot or another label. [LON, NYC08] **Owner decision (ORDER 20):** neighbouring labels keep at least 1 lw of clear space between them, so two names never read as one run. On desktop, OpenNews and Vox Media sit 1.5 lw further east (along time; the phone is unchanged) and the Vox Media label always wraps to two lines, which clears Palm Beach Post / OpenNews and OpenNews / Vox Media by at least 1 lw from 1280 to 1440. Medill / Palm Beach Post is still under 1 lw (open decision).
30. **A label starts about 0.5–1 lw past the outer edge of the bundle.** It goes on the side away from the nearest parallel line. Long names stack on two or three lines, flush toward the line. [NYC08, LON]
31. **Weight labels by role.** Interchange and terminal labels are bold and dark. Plain stations are regular weight and a lighter grey. [NYC08, NYC72]

### Legend

The London map has a "Key to lines" and a "Key to symbols" outside the map frame. These rules come from them.

32. **Include a key to lines.** One entry per line: a short horizontal swatch drawn at the map's line width, in the line's colour, with the line's name beside it. Keep the entry order the same every time. [LON]
33. **List the tracks under their line in the key.** Each track is listed once, with its letter (rule 22) and name (for example "A Finding stories"). Branches are not listed separately. London does not do this, so it is an addition of ours. [new] **Owner decision:** there is one key for the whole map. It lists all 12 tracks, grouped by line in ribbon order: journalism (A, B, C), code (D, E, F, G), leadership and outreach (K, L), then product (H, I, J). Its dots are the map's Ink dots with the cream ring.
34. **Include a key to symbols.** Show every kind of stop exactly as it is drawn on the map, with a text label each: station, terminal (a square with its track ending at the centre, rule 26), interchange, and the linked interchange if one is used. The symbols in the key are the same size as on the map. [LON]
35. **Draw part-time work as a solid track.** Part-time work is drawn as a solid track in the line colour, identical to full-time tracks: same width, same pitch (rules 10 and 11), same bundle rules. The map does not mark part-time, and the key has no part-time symbol. The station card says "part-time" for part-time stations. [owner decision; replaces hatching]
36. **Lay out the key as a left-aligned column.** Swatches or symbols sit in one column, and names sit at one fixed offset beside them. Group related entries under a short heading, and separate groups with a hairline rule. Each track appears once, as its letter and name. The key uses the same typeface as the map labels and sits outside the map frame. [LON]

### Themes and concurrent work

37. **Concurrent roles are branches.** A theme is one track. When two roles of the same theme overlap in time, the track splits (rule 5) at the latest stop before the parallel work starts, and each concurrent role gets its own branch. A branch never serves two stations at the same time. Where full-time roles do not overlap, one branch passes through them in order. Part-time gigs never chain: each part-time station gets its own branch per theme, which ends there (a terminal), unless the gig is ongoing (rule 27), in which case the branch runs off the edge. Branch assignment is by time, earliest first, and is recorded in `data/transit-map-data.json` (one entry per branch under `tracks`). [new, owner decision]
38. **One letter per track, no per-branch IDs.** A track's dots show its theme letter only, on every branch (rule 22). There are no per-branch suffixes such as A1, A2 or Aa, on the map or in the key. [new, owner decision; replaces numbered-plus-letter IDs, then theme numbers (ORDER 17)]

### Label content

39. **A station label is the station name only.** No subtitle, role, or years under or beside the name. The map shows no years or time axis at all: only the order of stations matters. Years appear only in the station card. Rules 29–31 still set placement and weight. [new, owner decision]

### Interaction

41. **Hover highlights the whole track.** With a mouse, hovering a track or any of its branches highlights the whole track and all its branches (hovering any part of track A highlights all of track A); moving the pointer off ends it. Mouse clicks never pin a highlight: a click on the map only selects a station, and a click on track away from a station does nothing. On touch, tapping a track toggles its theme on; tapping it again, or tapping anywhere else, clears it. Tapping a station dot still selects the station. While a track is highlighted, it, all its branches and their dots stay unchanged; every other track turns flat grey and every other dot turns grey with its ID still readable. An ongoing branch's dashed run (rule 27) highlights and greys with its track. Station labels where the theme stops stay Ink and bold; all other labels turn grey. In the key, the theme's row gets a Pink block with Ink text and other rows turn grey. Hovering a theme row in the key, or giving it keyboard focus, highlights the same way; leaving or blurring the row clears it. [new, owner decision]
